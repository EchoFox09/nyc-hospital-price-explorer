About this data

Where these prices come from, what they mean, and the limits of what they can tell you about your actual cost of care.

Where the data comes from

Since January 1, 2021, every U.S. hospital must publish a machine-readable file with the prices of all its services. This requirement comes from the federal CMS Hospital Price Transparency Rule (45 CFR § 180.50), intended to reduce the information asymmetry between hospitals and patients.

We downloaded these files from 10 NYC hospitals, normalized the data, and published it in the open Health graph on Geo Protocol. Relationships between procedures, hospitals, and prices are stored as primary data — the app reads them directly from the graph.

What's in the dataset

10
hospitals in NYC and the surrounding area
57
common procedures across 6 categories
556
price records — one per hospital × procedure pair

The hospitals were chosen for diversity: a public safety-net (Bellevue) within NYC Health + Hospitals, community safety-net hospitals (BronxCare, St. Barnabas), a major academic system (NYU Langone), and independent or smaller-system nonprofits (Brookdale, Kingsbrook, Englewood, Montefiore). This gives a realistic spectrum of the city’s pricing landscape.

Procedures are identified by CPT codes (Current Procedural Terminology) — the U.S. industry standard for billing medical services. CPT codes make any procedure directly comparable across different hospitals.

What each price means

GROSS CHARGE

Full chargemaster list price

The hospital’s sticker price — before any discounts or insurance negotiations. Almost no one actually pays this, but it’s the starting point for all other rates.

CASH PRICE

Price for uninsured patients

The amount the hospital will accept from a patient paying out of pocket. Often significantly lower than the gross charge.

MIN NEGOTIATED

Minimum insurer rate

The lowest amount any insurance company pays the hospital. Typically reflects contracts with major payers like Medicare and Medicaid.

MAX NEGOTIATED

Maximum insurer rate

The highest amount any insurance company pays the hospital. Often reflects out-of-network rates or less favorable contracts.

All four prices reflect only the hospital facility portion of the service: room, equipment, supplies, and the work of nurses and technicians. Physician professional fees are not included — they are billed separately and depend on insurance and other factors.

What's not included

  • Physician fees — for primary care doctors, surgeons, anesthesiologists, radiologists. Billed separately on their own invoice.
  • Drugs, implants, and supplies beyond standard — uncommon items in the CMS catalog.
  • Complications and extra inpatient days — depend on individual circumstances.
  • Emergency admission surcharges outside of a regular scheduled visit.

Limitations

Publication quality varies. Hospitals are required to publish data, but format and completeness differ. We normalized the data into a common model, but the source files may contain gaps or errors from the hospital itself.

This is a snapshot in time. Prices are published periodically and can change. Use this data as a reference, not as the final figure on your bill.

Your actual bill depends on your situation. Insurance plan, deductible, co-pay, in-network status, complications, length of stay — all affect the amount you personally pay. Before a scheduled procedure, request a good-faith estimate from the hospital — you have the right to one under the No Surprises Act.

Data in the graph

All data is published as entities and relations in an open knowledge graph on Geo Protocol. This means:

  • Anyone can verify the data directly, without intermediaries
  • Relationships (hospital ↔ procedure ↔ price) are stored as primary data — not pieced together after the fact
  • Versioning, authorship, and edit history are preserved
  • Other applications can read the same graph for their own purposes
